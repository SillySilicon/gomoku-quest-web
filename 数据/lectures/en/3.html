How to Block a Three

If your opponent makes a three, you must usually block it, as leaving it will allow them to create a straight four (a continuous line of four stones open on both ends).

Generally, for a broken three, block the middle. For a connected three, as the saying goes, "block the side it was made from" meaning it's better to block the direction from which the opponent just played.

Don't Waste Your Threes

Avoid making threes without a purpose.

Use your threes only when you have a clear goal, such as when making a three creates a chain reaction that allows you to form another three or a four, or when you can defend against the opponent's three or four while making your own (gaining the initiative).
